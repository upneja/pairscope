# Pairscope Scoring Rubrics

Scoring thresholds for all dimensions across the five validated frameworks. Each dimension is scored on a per-question average basis (mean of all items mapping to that dimension), except CSI-4 which uses a sum score, and Love Languages which use categorical tallying.

---

## 1. Gottman Four Horsemen

Each Horseman is scored as the **mean of all items** mapping to that dimension. Score range: 1.0 - 7.0.

### Criticism
| Threshold | Range | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Healthy | 1.0 - 2.9 | Uses specific complaints ("When X happens, I feel Y") rather than global character attacks. Conflict stays issue-focused. |
| At-Risk | 3.0 - 5.0 | Sometimes shifts from complaint to criticism under stress. May use "you always" or "you never" language when triggered. |
| Critical | 5.1 - 7.0 | Frequently frames issues as partner character flaws. Complaints escalate to attacks on the person, not the behavior. |

### Contempt
| Threshold | Range | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Healthy | 1.0 - 2.5 | Maintains fundamental respect even during disagreements. Does not engage in mockery, eye-rolling, or superiority displays. |
| At-Risk | 2.6 - 4.5 | Occasional superiority or dismissiveness during conflict. May use sarcasm as a weapon rather than humor. |
| Critical | 4.6 - 7.0 | Regular contempt signals (eye-rolling, mockery, disgust). **This is the single strongest predictor of relationship dissolution** in Gottman's research. Requires immediate attention. |

### Defensiveness
| Threshold | Range | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Healthy | 1.0 - 2.9 | Can hear feedback without counter-attacking or making excuses. Takes responsibility for their part in conflicts. |
| At-Risk | 3.0 - 5.0 | Sometimes deflects responsibility during conflict. May acknowledge partner's point but quickly pivots to self-justification. |
| Critical | 5.1 - 7.0 | Consistently avoids accountability. Meets every complaint with a counter-complaint. Blocks repair attempts. |

### Stonewalling
| Threshold | Range | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Healthy | 1.0 - 2.9 | Stays emotionally engaged during conflict, or uses healthy self-soothing breaks with a clear plan to return. |
| At-Risk | 3.0 - 5.0 | Occasionally shuts down during intense conflict. May withdraw without communicating the need for a break. |
| Critical | 5.1 - 7.0 | Frequently withdraws completely during conflict. Emotional flooding leads to disengagement. Partner feels abandoned mid-conversation. |

## 2. Gottman Sound Relationship House (SRH)

Each SRH dimension is scored as the **mean of all items** mapping to that dimension. Score range: 1.0 - 7.0. Note: higher scores are positive for SRH dimensions (unlike Horsemen, where higher = worse).

### Love Maps
| Threshold | Range | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Healthy | 5.0 - 7.0 | Strong knowledge of partner's inner world — their friends, stressors, dreams, and current concerns. |
| At-Risk | 3.0 - 4.9 | Some gaps in understanding partner's current state. May know the basics but miss evolving concerns. |
| Critical | 1.0 - 2.9 | Significant disconnection from partner's inner life. Lack of curiosity or updating of partner knowledge. |

### Fondness & Admiration
| Threshold | Range | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Healthy | 5.0 - 7.0 | Active respect and appreciation for partner. Can readily identify admirable qualities. |
| At-Risk | 3.0 - 4.9 | Fondness present but not regularly expressed. May take partner for granted. |
| Critical | 1.0 - 2.9 | Difficulty identifying admirable qualities in partner. Erosion of basic respect — a leading indicator of contempt. |

### Turning Toward
| Threshold | Range | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Healthy | 5.0 - 7.0 | Consistently responds to partner's bids for connection — both large ("Let's talk about our future") and small ("Look at this sunset"). |
| At-Risk | 3.0 - 4.9 | Sometimes misses or dismisses partner's bids. May be responsive when attentive but frequently distracted. |
| Critical | 1.0 - 2.9 | Frequently turns away from or against partner's bids. Emotional bank account is depleting. |

### Positive Sentiment Override
| Threshold | Range | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Healthy | 5.0 - 7.0 | Positive sentiment override active. Interprets ambiguous partner behavior charitably. Good memories outweigh bad. |
| At-Risk | 3.0 - 4.9 | Mixed sentiment. Sometimes assumes negative intent. May be in transition toward negative override. |
| Critical | 1.0 - 2.9 | Negative sentiment override. Assumes worst in partner's actions. Even neutral behavior is interpreted negatively. |

### Conflict Management
| Threshold | Range | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Healthy | 5.0 - 7.0 | Constructive conflict with active repair attempts and willingness to compromise. Can find humor mid-argument. |
| At-Risk | 3.0 - 4.9 | Some conflicts go unresolved. Mixture of avoidance and escalation patterns. Repair attempts are sometimes accepted. |
| Critical | 1.0 - 2.9 | Destructive conflict patterns. Gridlocked perpetual problems. Repair attempts are rarely made or accepted. |

### Shared Meaning
| Threshold | Range | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Healthy | 5.0 - 7.0 | Strong shared vision, purpose, and sense of building a life together. Aligned on core values and direction. |
| At-Risk | 3.0 - 4.9 | Some alignment but diverging life visions. May agree on surface goals but differ on deeper meaning. |
| Critical | 1.0 - 2.9 | Lack of shared purpose. Partners feel like they are on separate paths. No sense of "us" narrative. |

## 3. Attachment (ECR-R Based)

Each axis is scored as the **mean of all items** mapping to that dimension. Score range: 1.0 - 7.0.

### Attachment Anxiety
| Threshold | Range | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Secure | 1.0 - 2.5 | Low anxiety about partner's availability and love. Comfortable with normal separations and independence. |
| Moderate | 2.6 - 4.5 | Some worry about rejection or abandonment. May need more reassurance than average during stressful periods. |
| High | 4.6 - 7.0 | Significant preoccupation with partner's responsiveness. Fear of abandonment. May engage in protest behaviors (excessive texting, jealousy, testing). |

### Attachment Avoidance
| Threshold | Range | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Secure | 1.0 - 2.5 | Comfortable with closeness and emotional intimacy. Can depend on partner without discomfort. |
| Moderate | 2.6 - 4.5 | Some discomfort with deep emotional closeness. Values independence but can be vulnerable with effort. |
| High | 4.6 - 7.0 | Strong preference for independence. Discomfort with vulnerability. May suppress emotional needs or push partner away when they get too close. |

### Attachment Style Classification
Derived from the combination of anxiety and avoidance scores:

| Style | Anxiety | Avoidance | Description |
|-------|---------|-----------|-------------|
| Secure | Low (1.0-2.5) | Low (1.0-2.5) | Comfortable with intimacy and autonomy. The gold standard. |
| Anxious-Preoccupied | High (4.6-7.0) | Low (1.0-2.5) | Craves closeness, fears rejection. May come across as "clingy." |
| Dismissive-Avoidant | Low (1.0-2.5) | High (4.6-7.0) | Values independence, uncomfortable with emotional depth. May seem "cold." |
| Fearful-Avoidant | High (4.6-7.0) | High (4.6-7.0) | Wants closeness but fears it. Push-pull dynamic. Most complex style. |

Scores in the moderate range (2.6-4.5) on either axis suggest a leaning but not a dominant style. The report should describe tendencies rather than assigning a rigid label.

## 4. Big Five Personality Traits (BFI-K Based)

Each trait is scored as the **mean of all items** mapping to that dimension. Score range: 1.0 - 7.0.

### Neuroticism
| Threshold | Range | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Low | 1.0 - 2.9 | Emotionally stable. Recovers quickly from relationship stress. Does not ruminate. Research shows this is the strongest positive personality predictor of relationship satisfaction. |
| Moderate | 3.0 - 5.0 | Average emotional reactivity. Some rumination under stress but generally manageable. |
| High | 5.1 - 7.0 | High emotional reactivity. Tendency to worry, ruminate, and catastrophize. **The #1 negative predictor of relationship satisfaction** across the 9-year longitudinal study (N=972). This is the single most important personality-based risk factor. |

### Conscientiousness
| Threshold | Range | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| High | 5.0 - 7.0 | Reliable, follows through on commitments. **The #1 positive predictor of long-term relationship satisfaction.** Partners feel they can count on you. |
| Moderate | 3.0 - 4.9 | Generally reliable but inconsistent under pressure. May drop balls when stressed. |
| Low | 1.0 - 2.9 | Struggles with follow-through on promises and plans. Partner may feel they can't depend on you, which erodes trust over time. |

### Agreeableness
| Threshold | Range | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| High | 5.0 - 7.0 | Cooperative, empathic, trusting. Strong predictor of cross-sectional satisfaction (though effect washes out in long-term longitudinal studies). |
| Moderate | 3.0 - 4.9 | Balances cooperation with self-advocacy. Can compromise but also stands ground when needed. |
| Low | 1.0 - 2.9 | May struggle with compromise and empathy in conflict. Can come across as dismissive of partner's needs or feelings. |

## 5. Couples Satisfaction Index (CSI-4)

**Mode A only.** CSI-4 uses a **sum score** across 4 items, each scored 0-5. Total range: 0 - 20.

| Threshold | Range | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Satisfied | 13.5 - 20.0 | High relationship satisfaction. Strong emotional foundation. |
| Mixed | 8.0 - 13.4 | Some dissatisfaction present. Worth exploring which specific areas are contributing. |
| Distressed | 0 - 7.9 | Significant relationship distress. Below the clinical cutoff for notable dissatisfaction (original CSI-4 research uses 13.5 as the distress threshold). |

**Note:** The CSI-4 distress cutoff of 13.5 has strong convergent validity (.85-.98 correlation with the full 32-item DAS). Scores below this warrant attention even if other dimensions look healthy.

## 6. Love Languages

**No healthy/at-risk/critical thresholds.** Love languages are preference-based, not deficit-based.

### Scoring Method
- Each love language question maps one or more options to one of five categories: Words of Affirmation, Quality Time, Physical Touch, Acts of Service, Gifts.
- Responses are tallied across all love language items.
- The category with the highest tally is the user's **primary receiving love language**.
- A dedicated "giving" question identifies the **primary giving love language**.
- Ties are reported as co-primary languages.

### Key Insight: Mismatch Analysis
The diagnostic value of love languages in Pairscope comes not from the ranking itself, but from:
1. **Receiving vs. Giving mismatch** — When your giving language differs from your receiving language, your partner may feel unloved even though you're actively showing love (just in the wrong "language").
2. **Partner mismatch** (Mode A only, future couples feature) — When two partners have different primary languages, deposits into the emotional bank account are less efficient.

### Categories
| Language | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Words of Affirmation | Verbal expressions of love, appreciation, and encouragement |
| Quality Time | Focused, undivided attention and shared experiences |
| Physical Touch | Physical closeness, affection, and tactile connection |
| Acts of Service | Helpful actions that ease the partner's burden |
| Gifts | Thoughtful, symbolic tokens of love and attention |

## Scoring Implementation Notes

### Aggregation Method
For dimensions with multiple items, use the **arithmetic mean** of all item scores mapping to that dimension. This normalizes across dimensions with different item counts.

### Missing Data
If a user skips a question, exclude it from the mean calculation for that dimension. Flag dimensions where more than 50% of items are missing as "insufficient data."

### Score Normalization
All Likert and scenario scores are already on a 1-7 scale (or 0-5 for CSI). No additional normalization is required for threshold comparison.

### Emotional Bank Account Estimate
The Emotional Bank Account meter in the report is derived from a composite of:
- Turning Toward score (weight: 0.3)
- Positive Sentiment Override score (weight: 0.3)
- Fondness & Admiration score (weight: 0.2)
- Inverse of mean Horsemen scores (weight: 0.2)

Formula: `EBA = (0.3 * turning_toward + 0.3 * positive_sentiment + 0.2 * fondness_admiration + 0.2 * (8 - mean_horsemen)) / 7 * 100`

This yields a 0-100 percentage that maps to the visual meter in the report.
